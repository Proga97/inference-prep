When you don't know how to do a task

This will happen most days. It is not a signal that the plan is wrong or that you are behind — it is the actual work. What matters is that you escalate on a clock instead of grinding.

  1. 0–20 min · Name it precisely. Write the one sentence: "I don't know how to X" or "I expected A, got B." Then decide which kind of stuck it is — you don't know the concept, you don't know the API, or your code is simply broken. They have different fixes, and half the time writing the sentence solves it.
  2. 20–40 min · Go to the primary source. Every task's ▸ brief links the thing you actually need. Read the doc or the paper section, not a blog summary of it. For libraries, read the source and the tests — tests are the honest documentation.
  3. 40–60 min · Ask properly. Bring the error, your code, what you expected, and what you already tried. Ask why this happens, not fix this. A fix you can't explain becomes a resume bullet you can't defend — which is the exact failure this whole plan exists to undo.
  4. 60 min · Shrink it. Reproduce the problem in ten lines with everything else hardcoded. Most bugs die here, and the ones that don't become answerable questions.
  5. 90 min · Park it, don't grind. Write down exactly where you stopped and what you'd try next, hit ↻ to queue it, and move to the next task. Come back tomorrow with a fresh head, or take it to a human: a Purdue peer, the vLLM or SGLang Discord, the project's GitHub discussions.
  6. Stuck a second day · Cut scope. Ask the only question that matters: does the resume bullet or the interview answer actually require this? Usually not. Ship the simpler version and write the simplification into the README — "I did X instead of Y because Z" is itself strong interview material.
Two rules that matter more than the ladder. Never let one task eat a whole day — the build slot is 2.5 hours, and if 90 minutes in you have nothing, switching is the disciplined move, not the lazy one. And whatever confused you goes into the revision queue before you close the laptop; being confused is only wasted if you don't write it down.
